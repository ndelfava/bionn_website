Background

In biological brains, memory is consolidated largely offline during sleep. Recent activity is replayed and the synapses that carried it are strengthened or pruned. Adult neurogenesis adds a second mechanism: new neurons are introduced into a trained circuit and integrated slowly, so the circuit gains capacity without losing what it already encodes.

CTT borrows both ideas. The plastic layer is paused periodically, its neurons are scored, the least useful are removed, new neurons are introduced in their place, and the layer is renormalised before training resumes. What the score should measure is an open question. Scoring a neuron in isolation, by its own activation magnitude and gradient, says nothing about the role it plays in a circuit. A Hebbian score, computed from co-activation with its inputs, is intended to capture that.

Aims

  • Determine whether Hebbian-scored turnover applied during simulated sleep cycles produces more robust memory consolidation than scoring neurons in isolation.
  • Compare CTT against regularisation and stochastic masking baselines on the same continual task sequence.
  • Establish whether experience replay, elastic weight consolidation and synaptic scaling are sufficient on their own for this sequence.

Method

Task sequence

An LSTM foraging agent is trained through a sequence of four seasons: Summer, Autumn, Winter and Spring. Each season has its own resource availability and survival pressure. The agent sees each season once, in order, and the final model is then evaluated on all four. A model that has forgotten an earlier season cannot survive it.

Sleep cycles

Training is punctuated by offline phases that stand in for sleep. During a sleep cycle no new experience is collected. Stored trajectories are replayed, neurons are scored, turnover is applied, and homeostatic scaling restores the activity statistics of the layer.

Conditions

LSTM BaseUnmodified recurrent policy.
DropoutStochastic masking of activations.
ZoneoutStochastic retention of the hidden state.
CTT_NormalTurnover using the isolation scorer.
CTT_GHOSTTurnover using the Hebbian (GHOST) scorer.

All conditions also run with experience replay, elastic weight consolidation and synaptic scaling, so CTT is compared against an existing continual learning stack rather than against an unprotected baseline.

The two scorers

Each neuron i in the plastic layer receives a score. The lowest-scoring fraction is killed at the next turnover event.

Results

The baseline, dropout and zoneout models survive only the season they were trained on last. Every earlier season sits at 0%. Both CTT variants retain non-zero survivability in Autumn and Winter, which is direct evidence that turnover preserves something the other architectures lose.

The difference between the two scorers is small and inconsistent. GHOST is ahead in Autumn and Spring and behind in Winter. Summer is unsolved by every condition, which points to a property of the task rather than of the architecture.

Findings

  • CTT showed evidence of remembering. It is the only intervention tested that keeps earlier seasons alive in the final model.
  • Scorers are second order to architecture. Swapping the isolation scorer for GHOST moves survivability far less than adding turnover does.
  • Replay, elastic weight consolidation and synaptic scaling were not sufficient for this task sequence. The stack still collapses to the most recent season.
  • Future work should focus on new algorithms for continual reinforcement learning task sequences rather than on further refinements of the scoring function.
